Turns an architect's or interior designer's renders into a short cinematic film across seasons, times of day and moods. The building you drew is the one that appears.

Open the tool See how it works Source

Runs in the browser. Nothing to install, no account, no server, and nothing you upload leaves your machine. Start a sample project on the first screen draws two renders in the tab and fills in the brief, so you can see the whole thing work before finding your own files.

The problem it exists for

A practice sells design intent to homeowners, contractors, planning officers and awards juries.

Image models will happily invent a staircase, move a road or rearrange a kitchen while changing the season. That is fine for a mood board and fatal for a client presentation. This engine treats your uploaded renders as the source of truth and changes only light, weather, season, occupancy and framing.

The renders are sacred

No added floors, moved walls, swapped finishes or rearranged furniture. Exteriors and interiors each carry their own protected-element list.

Hub and spoke, never a chain

Every generated image references an original upload. Nothing is ever generated from a previous generation, because drift compounds at every hop.

Measured, not eyeballed

Every clip is scored on eight metrics before you see it. A failure is named with its cause, not hidden behind a nice thumbnail.

A visualisation, not a study

Sun direction is approximated from a stated compass heading. Every deliverable carries a disclaimer and an optional project-stage stamp.

Why drift matters

The single rule that separates a usable film from an expensive mood board.

By the third hop a chained pipeline has invented a staircase and re-routed the road. The engine refuses to work that way: if a shot needs an angle no original covers, it generates from the nearest original and tells you the risk in writing.

What you get at the end

The film

Hard cuts at 30 fps, title cards, a stage stamp and disclaimer, an ambience bed, plus crop-only variants for other aspect ratios. The server version normalises the bed to −16 LUFS with ffmpeg; the browser reports an RMS approximation and says so.

The stills pack

Every approved still at full resolution with sidecar metadata, because a practice reuses them in decks, reports and planning packs.

The project record

Every hub render, prompt, generation id, audit deviation, QC table, approval and the total spend. The file copy for when a client asks in six months why the film showed the deck in rain.
